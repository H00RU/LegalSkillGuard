# Learning Reader Interfaces and Validating Skill Updates under Shared Interpretations

## Abstract

External legal skills let a frozen language model apply reusable rules, provided it binds source facts to the right parties, claims, or clauses. We study consumer-preserving synthesis of reader interfaces and supervision that checks whether one admissible interpretation explains a proposed skill's outputs across related cases. After training on 128 sources, we evaluated five matched conditions and a static initial library on 160 held-out sources across five datasets. On LegalBench Diversity, synthesized interfaces raised balanced accuracy from 70.83% to 83.33% in both factorial comparisons and against the locked PARSE control. The paired 95% interval for the 12.50-percentage-point difference spanned −5.00 to 30.00. Source audits verified all six repairs and found unsupported claim relations in all five regressions. Shared-interpretation supervision yielded identical held-out predictions in both factorial comparisons. The results identify source-grounded repairs from interface synthesis while leaving its held-out net effect uncertain.

## Introduction

Legal reasoning tasks often depend on facts that refer to a particular party, item, claim, or clause. An amount must attach to the correct claim, and an exception must attach to the obligation it qualifies. A legal skill can compute the right result from these facts and still fail when a fixed model reads the wrong relationship. The same distinction matters during skill updates. A mistake observed on a single source may reflect a faulty reader, a faulty rule, or an unresolved fact that admits more than one interpretation.

We study the interface seen by the reader and the evidence used to accept a skill update. We derive candidate interfaces from an executable legal consumer and check equivalence on the declared domain. Actual paired reads then identify which interface is worth carrying to another source. For updates, we generate source variants under independently audited constraints. We measure native gains and test whether one shared assignment explains the outputs across the variants. In the paired conditioned comparison, a shared-specific veto requires a compatible incumbent under the same measurements. These mechanisms act at different points in the same external skill pipeline.

The study spans five benchmark families, with source mothers as the unit of comparison. We measure development performance at five training checkpoints and evaluate the frozen final libraries on an independently held-out cohort. We report native metrics for each family alongside execution routes and audits of source support.

## Related work

**Reader interfaces and policy programs.** Schema optimization adapts the extraction task to the reader. PARSE rewrites extraction schemas and compiles mappings back to the requested output format. Neurosymbolic policy compilation also lets a small reader handle narrow observations. SCOPE combines neural predicates with deterministic extractors and executable composition, and uses counterexamples to improve the program. We derive candidate relations from a fixed legal consumer, certify which distinctions their decoders must preserve, and choose among those relations using source-audited reads. [PARSE [3]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-industry.184/) · [SCOPE [4]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31062)

**Evidence for skill updates.** SkillEvoReg uses variations targeted to proposed skill changes to compare a candidate with its incumbent. In tax software, Synedrion uses higher-order metamorphic tests to compare outputs under structured changes to cases. We derive the allowed output tuples from independently audited source constraints. An unresolved factual value can take several admissible values, and related cases must share one assignment to every unedited fact within the declared scope. This gives the update checker a comparison even when individual variants lack unique reference answers. [SkillEvoReg [5]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30861) · [Synedrion [6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13471)

## Methods

The study keeps the language model fixed and learns external components at two stages of legal reasoning. The first stage changes the representation that a model reads from the source, with a decoder certified against the original consumer. The second stage evaluates a proposed skill update on source variants that share legal interpretations and unedited case facts. Both stages preserve the original benchmark outputs.

**Method overview.** M1 certifies equivalent consumer outputs before comparing candidate readers. M2 derives allowed output tuples for multiple source variants under one shared assignment. The Diversity and OpenExempt panels show how the two checks work on illustrative inputs. [Vector figure](figures/METHOD_OVERVIEW.pdf)

### Consumer-preserving interface learning

Let $F(s,c)$ be a fixed consumer of local source facts $s$ in context $c$. A candidate contains a representation $H$ and a decoder $D$. On the enumerated legal domain, the compiler checks

$$
D(H(s),c)=F(s,c).
$$

The comparison covers the complete registered output signature and any protected source distinctions. If two admissible states produce the same encoding and lead to different consumer outputs, the compiler records the pair and rejects the candidate. This criterion admits different representations of the same legal computation. In the diversity-jurisdiction tasks, for example, a local interface can represent whether two named parties have different citizenship states and whether claims attached to a single plaintiff–defendant pair cross the amount threshold. Different parties and claim pairs retain separate anchors.

The compiler extracts comparisons, arithmetic expressions, and Boolean composition from a restricted consumer syntax. The decoder resolves the finite representation without access to native annotations. Unknown source attributes remain unknown whenever the preserved output depends on them. For local contract scope, Boolean candidates combine evidence about the target slots of a named exception. The wire schema enumerates the certified joint support when independent field domains admit impossible tuples. Training compares real reads of the candidates on paired source mothers. A native score contributes to selection when the source audit also supports the model's observations. The library records the source-independent reader template and executor version, and it selects a template for a new source before that source's reading or label arrives.

### Skill-update validation under shared interpretations

A source group consists of cases assembled from explicit source edits. The compiler recomputes the derived quantities and relations affected by each edit. One interpretation of a law or definition is shared within its declared legal scope. An unresolved factual attribute is shared among variants of its source mother and receives a separate variable for each independent mother.

Let $Q=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ be a group, $\Omega_K$ the independently audited set of compatible assignments, and $R_\omega$ the reference projection under assignment $\omega$. The permitted signatures are

$$
\Sigma_K(Q)=\{(R_\omega(x_1),\ldots,R_\omega(x_k)):\omega\in\Omega_K\}.
$$

For every measured candidate, each group contributes a finite factor over the assignments that support its asserted outputs. A join over these factors determines whether one assignment remains compatible with the full observed prefix. The selector can identify a contradiction that is invisible when examining groups separately. An explicit abstention leaves the corresponding value unobserved. The base selection criterion is a paired native gain. The shared-specific veto applies to an incompatible proposal when the incumbent has a compatible witness under the same measurement contract. The ordinary and shared selectors compare the same proposed programs, source inputs, executor responses, and native labels. Independent completions and a fixed single witness serve as additional controls on the same signatures.

A local output can express either an interpretation-dependent projection or a claim that the literal source establishes a fact. Compatibility with one explanation suffices for the first check. A literal assertion requires support under every audited interpretation. For example, even when one common ownership assignment explains a pair of predictions, those predictions cannot serve as evidence that the text establishes ownership if another admissible assignment denies it.

### Data and execution

The sealed roster comprises OpenExempt [7], ContractNLI [8], MAUD [2], RuleArena Airline [9], and the LegalBench Diversity task family [1]. It contains 128 training sources in eight fixed batches, 80 development sources, and 160 test sources. The matching unit is the source mother. The development pilot takes four training mothers per dataset from this roster. The held-out identities are checked against the historical exposure index and the continuous request ledger. Development diagnostics and controlled variants retain their parent identity and are recorded outside the independent test denominator. The local reading comparisons use the same frozen Qwen3-8B checkpoint with thinking disabled. The teacher is invoked through the Codex CLI with an explicit gpt-6.1-sol model and high reasoning effort. We draw the clauses for the contract-scope diagnostics from MAUD and audit the selected clauses under our source review protocol.

### Evaluation protocol

The study asks five questions. **RQ1** asks how each method changes native task performance against matched external skill learning. **RQ2** asks how those changes develop as the training prefix grows. **RQ3** asks whether a synthesized representation improves source-grounded reading over fixed fields and format-only changes. **RQ4** asks when shared constraints change skill selection relative to native scores, one admissible interpretation, and independent completions. **RQ5** asks whether a learned skill helps beyond its source dataset.

The main design pairs five systems on the same training history. The first learns teacher skills using native supervision. The next three add interface learning, shared-interpretation supervision, or both. The last uses the strongest prespecified external adaptation measured on the fixed training pilot before the main study. The systems share the initial program, frozen executor, source scope, and comparable learning budgets. The 20-mother pilot ranked the PARSE-style adaptation first on the prespecified equal-dataset selection utility (0.567621). The SCOPE-style adaptation scored 0.533015, and the SkillEvoReg-style and Synedrion-style controls tied at 0.516349. We use the selected PARSE-style adaptation as the sole external arm throughout the formal matrix. The component comparisons keep each candidate pool and scored inputs fixed where the research question allows. In the update comparisons, the native and relation-aware selectors can reuse the exact same candidate responses.

The design evaluates the same development cohort at B0, B1, B2, B4, and B8. B8 is the prescribed test checkpoint across all five datasets. The final evaluation also scores the frozen common B0 library as an inference reference on the same test sources. The cross-dataset comparison at B8 removes skills learned exclusively on other datasets while preserving the shared initialization and the retrieval policy. Source-derived cases share the same independent sample unit as their mothers. An unhandled or unresolved native unit remains part of the score denominator. OpenExempt EC and EV receive separate official F1 measures. ContractNLI reports three-way classification and evidence F1. MAUD reports native classification by task, Airline reports exact total-cost accuracy, and Diversity reports accuracy and balanced accuracy. Paired uncertainty intervals resample source mothers, keeping all their native tasks together.

The formal training schedule used 733 physical executor calls with 5,031,094 tokens and 80 teacher calls with 1,411,141 tokens. The five scheduled development checkpoints added 393 distinct executor calls and 2,872,390 tokens. These counts charge each original physical call once, even if several arms or checkpoints used its response. Exploratory training probes are itemized separately.
